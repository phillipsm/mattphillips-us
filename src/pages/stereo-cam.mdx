# Stereoscopic Camera

<p class="lede">An investigation in digital stereoscopic photography.</p>

<img src="/assets/stereo-cam/stereo-cam-proj.jpg" alt="The stereoscopic camera in action — three sheets of acrylic sandwiching a four-camera circuit." width="860" height="548" />

## The Premise

We see in stereo. Two eyes, two slightly offset images, a brain that fuses them into something rich with depth and texture. Cameras can see the same way — they just need more lenses.

This camera has four. They fire simultaneously when the shutter is pressed, capturing four images of the same moment from four slightly different vantage points. On paper, those frames could be combined with [lenticular printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenticular_printing). On the web, an animated GIF gets you something similar — a flickering, parallax-rich view of an ordinary scene.

## How It's Built

The prototype is four cameras wired to an Arduino microcontroller, writing to an SD card on shutter release. The enclosure is a three-layer acrylic sandwich, laser-cut: front plate with four lens holes, middle plate to seat the electronics, back plate exposing the shutter button and battery.
